# SADAK — demo video script

Runs about 3 minutes. Live at https://main.d33zh3b90nj4kw.amplifyapp.com

## The story this tells

Not a feature tour. One person with one problem, followed from start to finish:
**you have moved to Hyderabad, you don't speak Telugu, and tomorrow you have to
buy vegetables.** Every click in the video is that person solving that problem.

It matters because a feature tour asks the viewer to imagine who would use this.
A story shows them. The market haggle lands completely differently when it is
the thing you said at the start you were afraid of.

Use your own version if the details are different — moved for college, moved for
work, visiting family. Keep it first person and keep it true. The narration
below is written the way people actually speak, with the hesitations left in.
Read it a few times, then say it your own way.

---

## Before you record

1. **Play the demo through once, start to finish.** Every spoken line is
   generated the first time and cached after. One pass makes every later take
   play instantly instead of pausing.
2. **Compile the railway world in a second tab and leave it sitting there.** The
   railway beat is a ten-second glance. Building it on camera costs you twenty
   dead seconds and kills the pace.
3. **Test the microphone in the browser you are recording in.** Chrome asks
   permission once, and it will choose the middle of your best take.
4. **Sign out**, so you open on the login screen.
5. **Load the site once** a few minutes before. It is serverless, so a cold
   instance makes the first page slow and it will look like the app is heavy.
6. Close other tabs. The 3D world and the recorder both want the GPU.
7. Record 1080p or better — the Telugu on the dialogue card has to be legible.

## Beat 1 · The problem, told as yours — 0:00 to 0:25

**Screen:** Login page. Nothing moving. Let it sit while you talk.

> A while ago I moved to Hyderabad. I can read a menu now. I still can't buy
> vegetables.
>
> There's a stall at the end of my road and I've walked past it more times than
> I want to admit, because the woman there opens with a sentence in Telugu and
> by the time I've worked out the shape of it she's already serving somebody
> else.

**Screen:** Move to **Continue as guest** and click.

> So this is what I built for that. It opens as a guest — nothing to sign up
> for. Which matters, because the people who need this are not going to create
> an account to find out whether it's any good.

**Delivery:** Slowest part of the video. This is the only stretch where you are
asking someone to care rather than showing them something. The detail about
walking past the stall is what makes it real — don't trim it.

---

## Beat 2 · Choosing a city, and choosing yourself — 0:25 to 0:55

**Screen:** The ten city cards. Move across Hyderabad, Amritsar, Kolkata as you
name them.

> Ten cities, and each one speaks its own language. Hyderabad is Telugu.
> Amritsar is Punjabi. Kolkata is Bengali. You're not picking a difficulty
> setting — you're picking a place.

**Screen:** Scroll to the second picker. Rest the cursor on the language list
for a beat before you speak.

> And then this, which is the part I care about most.
>
> You tell it what you already understand. Every hint, every meaning, every
> correction comes back in that language. I read English, so I'll take English.
>
> But my neighbour reads Telugu and Hindi and no English at all. He can learn
> Hindi here without English ever being in the room. Most apps quietly assume
> English is the floor you start from. For most of this country, it isn't.

**Screen:** Pick Hyderabad, pick a level, click **Enter**.

> Hyderabad. English. Let's go.

**Delivery:** The neighbour line is worth saying properly. It takes eight
seconds and it is the difference between an app for people who already speak
English and an app for everyone else.

---

## Beat 3 · Two doors — 0:55 to 1:15

**Screen:** The screen offering **Just walk in** and the describe box.

> Now it asks what I actually want to do, and there are two answers that are
> quite different from each other.

**Screen:** Point at **Just walk in**.

> One — just walk in. There's a street already built for Hyderabad, with errands
> written for it. That's for when I want general practice.

**Screen:** Move to the describe box. Type slowly enough to be read:
`I want to buy vegetables at the market`

> Two, and this is the one that's different. I type what's actually happening
> tomorrow. In English, in one line.

**Screen:** Don't submit. Click **Just walk in**.

> I'll come back to that. City first.

**Delivery:** Don't rush the typing. Let people read it appearing. The pause
before you click away sets up the payoff in beat 5.

---

## Beat 4 · The street — 1:15 to 1:45

**Screen:** The world loads. Walk a few steps so it is obviously live and not
footage.

> This is Charminar Lane. It's a street, and I walk down it.

**Screen:** Approach a character until the dialogue opens.

> There are people on it, and every one of them speaks Telugu. Only Telugu.
> There's no English fallback, no button for a translation.

**Screen:** Hold the mic, say your line in Telugu, release. Let her reply play
out loud, all the way.

> *(nothing — let it play)*

**Screen:** Point at the three lines under her reply.

> So she's said something, and I get three things back. The Telugu. How to
> actually say it out loud. And what it means — in English, because that's what
> I told it I read.

**Screen:** Point at the card underneath.

> And then this line, which is the one I use most. What I could say back. Not a
> word list. The actual next sentence in this particular conversation.

**Delivery:** Stay silent while she answers. Every instinct will tell you to
narrate over it. Don't — three seconds of a Telugu seller replying to you is
worth more than any sentence you could lay on top.

**If speech fails:** there's a keyboard toggle on the card. Type it and keep
moving. Retrying the mic on camera looks worse than typing.

---

## Beat 5 · Tomorrow, rehearsed — 1:45 to 2:35

**Screen:** Exit back to the two doors.

> Right. Now the thing I said I'd come back to.

**Screen:** Type `I want to buy vegetables at the market` and submit.

> Tomorrow I'm going to the market for vegetables. So I tell it that. One line,
> in English, no menu, no template.

**Screen:** It compiles. Let the wait breathe — say this over it, not quickly.

> And it's building the place. Not loading a level that was already sitting
> there — working out what a vegetable market on this street actually contains.
> Who's selling. What they have. What they charge. What I need to come away
> with.

**Screen:** The market loads.

> There it is.

**Screen:** Walk to the seller. Open the dialogue. Point at the errand panel.

> And there's the errand — a kilo of tomatoes. She wants forty rupees a kilo.
> This is the exact thing I said at the start that I can't do.

**Screen:** Speak. Haggle. Three or four turns. Say nothing.

> *(quiet, all the way through)*

**Screen:** Point at the wallet as the number changes.

> She came down to thirty-five. And look — the money actually left my wallet.
>
> That number isn't the model being generous. The engine decides what she's
> allowed to charge and what she's got left, and the model only ever chooses
> words. That's the whole design: it controls how she talks, never what's true.

**Delivery:** Your strongest fifty seconds — give them room. Two things do the
work: the callback to beat 1, and the wallet changing. A model that cannot
invent a price is the line between a simulation and a chatbot in costume, and
you can point at the proof on screen.

---

## Beat 6 · Not just markets — 2:35 to 2:50

**Screen:** Switch to the tab holding the railway world.

> And it isn't only markets. Next week I've got a train.

**Screen:** Show the ticket counter and the errands panel.

> Same one line of English, completely different world. A ticket to buy, a
> platform to find, somebody to ask.

**Delivery:** Fifteen seconds, then move. This beat only exists to prove the
last one wasn't a one-off.

---

## Beat 7 · Close — 2:50 to 3:05

**Screen:** Back on the street, walking.

> That's it. Ten languages, my own voice, and a world built out of one sentence
> I typed in English.

**Screen:** Let the address bar show for a moment.

> It's live on AWS — Amplify running the app, DynamoDB holding the worlds it
> builds.

**Screen:** Keep walking as you land the last line.

> The link opens as a guest. Go and fail at buying tomatoes yourself.

**Delivery:** Say the last line lightly. It works because it's self-deprecating
and it closes the loop you opened in beat 1.

## If you're over time

Cut in this order:

1. **Beat 6, the railway world.** Fifteen seconds, and breadth is the cheapest
   thing to lose.
2. **The neighbour sentence in beat 2.** Painful, but it's eight seconds.
3. **The AWS line in beat 7.** The submission form already says where it runs.
4. **Trim beat 1** to the first two sentences.

Never cut the second language picker, the describe box, or the haggle. Those
three are the product, and the haggle is the only place the viewer sees the
engine and the model doing different jobs.

## What you can claim

All true, all visible in the recording:

- ten Indian cities, ten languages
- you speak out loud, the characters answer out loud
- a 3D world generated from one sentence of plain English
- the engine sets prices and stock; the model can't invent either
- deployed on AWS, Amplify and DynamoDB
- no sign-up

**Leave Bedrock out.** The integration is written and one environment variable
from running, but this AWS account's Bedrock quotas are set to zero, so the
recording runs on Groq. If a judge asks what the reasoning runs on, the true
answer is a good one — a provider seam with a tested fallback, on Groq because
Bedrock access hasn't come through — but it isn't a claim to put in a video.

## When something breaks mid-take

**The world won't compile.** Reload and use the authored Hyderabad bazaar. The
haggle plays out the same and nobody watching knows which one you meant.

**Speech isn't recognised.** Keyboard toggle, keep talking. Don't stop to
explain.

**No audio.** Cache is cold. Press "hear it" once and re-record the beat.

**First page load is slow.** Serverless cold start — load the site a few minutes
before you record.

**You fluff a line.** Keep rolling and say it again. Cutting one bad sentence in
an edit is easier than restarting a three-minute take.
