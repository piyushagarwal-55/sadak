# SADAK — demo video script

Runs about 3 minutes 10. Live at https://main.d33zh3b90nj4kw.amplifyapp.com

## The story this tells

One person, one situation, followed the whole way through: **you're hoping to
join Amazon, that means moving to Hyderabad, and you don't speak Telugu.**

The frame does real work. You are not someone who already moved and is stuck —
you are someone getting ready for something that hasn't happened yet. That is
exactly what SADAK is for, and it makes the describe-a-scenario feature the
natural centre of the video instead of a clever extra. When you type "I want to
buy vegetables at the market", you are rehearsing your first Saturday there.

Amazon's largest campus outside the US really is in Hyderabad, so the setup
holds up if anyone checks.

Say it in your own words. The narration below is written the way people talk.
Read it a few times, then stop reading it.

---

## Before you record

1. **Play the demo through once, start to finish.** Every spoken line is
   generated the first time and cached after. One pass makes every later take
   play instantly instead of stalling.
2. **Compile the railway world in a second tab and leave it open.** That beat is
   a ten-second look. Building it on camera costs twenty dead seconds.
3. **Test the microphone in the browser you're recording in.** Chrome asks
   permission once and it will pick your best take to do it.
4. **Sign out**, so you open on the landing page rather than the city picker.
5. **Load the site a few minutes before.** It's serverless — a cold instance
   makes the first load slow and it reads as the app being heavy.
6. Close other tabs. The 3D world and the recorder both want the GPU.
7. 1080p minimum. The Telugu on the dialogue card has to be readable.

## Beat 1 · The setup — 0:00 to 0:30

**Screen:** The landing page, at the top. A darkened Hyderabad street with
**SADAK.** across it. Don't move the cursor while you talk — let the hero sit.

> Let's say I get into Amazon. That means Hyderabad — their biggest campus
> outside the US is there. So, naturally, I'd have to pick up some Telugu.

*(short pause)*

> And I've been thinking about what that actually looks like. It isn't the
> interview I'm worried about. It's the first Saturday, standing at a vegetable
> stall, not able to say a word.

**Screen:** Scroll slowly — one flick — into the city panels. Hover one and let
it expand.

> Ten cities. Each one speaks its own language.

**Screen:** Click **Continue as guest** in the hero, or the button in the nav.

> So this is what I built for that. No account — it opens as a guest.

**Delivery:** Slowest stretch in the video, and the only part where you're asking
someone to care rather than showing them something. The pause before "And I've
been thinking" is worth taking.

The scroll is worth five seconds on its own. Those panels expanding is the first
moment the video looks like a product rather than a school project, and it costs
you nothing to show it before you click through.

---

## Beat 2 · Where I'm going, and what I already know — 0:30 to 0:58

**Screen:** You're through the guest button and on the city picker now. Move
across Hyderabad, Amritsar, Kolkata as you name them.

> Hyderabad is Telugu. Amritsar is Punjabi. Kolkata is Bengali. I'm not picking
> a difficulty level here — I'm picking where I'm going.

**Screen:** Scroll to the second picker. Rest the cursor there for a beat before
speaking.

> And then this one, which is the part I care about most.
>
> You tell it what you already understand. Every hint, every meaning, every
> correction comes back in that language. I read English, so I'll take English.
>
> But a friend of mine reads Hindi and Telugu and no English at all. He can
> learn Hindi here without English ever being in the room. Most apps quietly
> assume English is the floor you start from. For most of this country, it
> isn't.

**Screen:** Pick Hyderabad, pick a level, click **Enter**.

> Hyderabad. English. Let's go.

**Delivery:** The friend sentence takes eight seconds and it's the difference
between an app for people who already speak English and an app for everyone
else. Don't throw it away.

## Beat 3 · Two ways in — 0:58 to 1:18

**Screen:** The screen offering **Just walk in** and the describe box.

> Now it asks what I actually want to do, and there are two answers that are
> quite different.

**Screen:** Point at **Just walk in**.

> One — just walk in. There's a street already built for Hyderabad with errands
> written for it. That's for when I want general practice in the language.

**Screen:** Move to the describe box. Type slowly enough to be read:
`I want to buy vegetables at the market`

> Two, and this is the different one. I type what I'm actually going to be
> doing. In English. One line.

**Screen:** Don't submit. Click **Just walk in**.

> I'll come back to that. City first.

**Delivery:** Let people watch the sentence appear. The deliberate walk-away is
what makes beat 5 land as a payoff rather than a new topic.

---

## Beat 4 · The street — 1:18 to 1:48

**Screen:** The world loads. Walk a few steps so it's obviously live, not
footage.

> This is Charminar Lane. It's a street, and I walk down it.

**Screen:** Approach a character until the dialogue opens.

> There are people on it, and every one of them speaks Telugu. Only Telugu. No
> English fallback, no button for a translation.

**Screen:** Hold the mic, say your line in Telugu, release. Let the reply play
all the way out.

> *(nothing — let it play)*

**Screen:** Point at the three lines under her reply.

> So she's said something, and I get three things back. The Telugu. How to
> actually say it out loud. And what it means — in English, because that's what
> I told it I read.

**Screen:** Point at the card underneath.

> And then this line, which is the one I'd use most. What I could say back. Not
> a word list — the actual next sentence in this conversation.

**Delivery:** Stay silent while she answers. Every instinct says narrate over
it. Don't. Three seconds of a Telugu seller replying to you beats anything you
could say on top.

**If speech fails:** there's a keyboard toggle on the card. Type it, keep going.
Retrying the mic on camera looks worse than typing.

---

## Beat 5 · Rehearsing the first Saturday — 1:48 to 2:38

**Screen:** Exit back to the two doors.

> Right. The thing I said I'd come back to.

**Screen:** Type `I want to buy vegetables at the market` and submit.

> First weekend after I land, I'm going to have to buy vegetables. So I tell it
> that. One line, in English — no menu, no template.

**Screen:** It compiles. Let the wait breathe; say this over it, unhurried.

> And it's building the place. Not loading a level that was already sitting
> there — working out what a vegetable market on this street actually contains.
> Who's selling. What they have. What they charge. What I need to walk away
> with.

**Screen:** The market loads.

> There it is.

**Screen:** Walk to the seller, open the dialogue, point at the errand panel.

> And there's the errand — a kilo of tomatoes. She's asking forty rupees a kilo.
> This is the exact thing I said I was worried about, and I get to be bad at it
> here first.

**Screen:** Speak. Haggle. Three or four turns. Say nothing.

> *(quiet, all the way through)*

**Screen:** Point at the wallet as the number changes.

> She came down to thirty-five. And look — the money actually left my wallet.
>
> That number isn't the model being generous. The engine decides what she's
> allowed to charge and what she's got left, and the model only ever picks
> words. That's the whole design: it controls how she talks, never what's true.

**Delivery:** Your strongest fifty seconds. Two things carry it — the callback
to beat 1, and the wallet changing on screen. A model that can't invent a price
is the line between a simulation and a chatbot in costume, and you can point at
the proof.

---

## Beat 6 · Not only markets — 2:38 to 2:53

**Screen:** Switch to the tab holding the railway world.

> And it isn't only markets. At some point I'll have to get on a train.

**Screen:** Show the ticket counter and the errands panel.

> Same one line of English, completely different world. A ticket to buy, a
> platform to find, somebody to ask.

**Delivery:** Fifteen seconds, then move on. This beat only exists to prove the
last one wasn't a one-off.

---

## Beat 7 · Close — 2:53 to 3:08

**Screen:** Back on the street, walking.

> So — ten languages, my own voice, and a world built out of one sentence I
> typed in English.

**Screen:** Let the address bar show for a moment.

> It's live on AWS. Amplify running the app, DynamoDB holding the worlds it
> builds.

**Screen:** Keep walking as you land it.

> The link opens as a guest. Go and try to buy tomatoes.

**Delivery:** Last line light, slightly dry. It closes the loop you opened in
beat 1 and it doesn't oversell.

## If you're over time

Cut in this order:

1. **Beat 6, the railway world.** Fifteen seconds, and breadth is the cheapest
   thing to lose.
2. **The friend sentence in beat 2.** Painful, but it's eight seconds.
3. **The AWS line in beat 7.** The submission form already says where it runs.
4. **Trim beat 1** to the first two sentences.

Never cut the second language picker, the describe box, or the haggle. Those
three are the product, and the haggle is the only moment a viewer sees the
engine and the model doing different jobs.

## What you can claim

All true and visible in the recording:

- ten Indian cities, ten languages
- you speak out loud, characters answer out loud
- a 3D world generated from one sentence of plain English
- the engine sets prices and stock; the model can't invent either
- deployed on AWS, Amplify and DynamoDB
- no sign-up

**Leave Bedrock out.** The integration is written and one environment variable
from running, but this account's Bedrock quotas are set to zero, so the
recording runs on Groq. If a judge asks what the reasoning runs on, the honest
answer is a good one — a provider seam with a tested fallback, currently Groq
because Bedrock access hasn't come through. It just isn't a claim to put in a
video.

## When something breaks mid-take

**The world won't compile.** Reload and use the authored Hyderabad bazaar. The
haggle plays the same and nobody watching knows which one you meant.

**Speech isn't recognised.** Keyboard toggle, keep talking, don't explain.

**No audio.** Cache is cold — press "hear it" once, then re-record the beat.

**First load is slow.** Serverless cold start. Load the site a few minutes
before you record.

**You fluff a line.** Keep rolling and say it again. Cutting one sentence in an
edit beats restarting a three-minute take.
